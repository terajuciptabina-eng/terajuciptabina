REAL-WORLD PROBLEM SCENARIOS

When cost, progress and payment are not connected, both sides can lose visibility.

Two real construction scenarios illustrate the underlying problem TERAJU WORKS is designed to address. These are problem scenarios, not claims about TERAJU WORKS customers.

CASE 01 · CLUSTER HOUSE · TAMAN DESIRAN BAYU

RM250k lump-sum contract

  • • Quotation existed, but components were priced as a lump sum.
  • • Contractor claimed 50%, then another 30%, without a clear item-level basis.
  • • Homeowner had paid more than 95% while physical progress was below 70%.
  • • VO and scope changes were difficult to reconcile with the original budget.
  • • Example: SPC had not yet been purchased or installed, yet the homeowner could not clearly determine whether its cost had already been absorbed in earlier claims.
  • • Project eventually reached a funding gap before completion.
CASE 02 · BUNGALOW · PENANG

RM300k budget → RM420k spent

  • • Original project budget was RM300,000.
  • • RM420,000 had already been spent — RM120,000 above the original budget (+40%).
  • • Contractor provided a total figure without detailed cost breakdown.
  • • Claims were made by percentage rather than identifiable work items.
  • • VO and additional costs were not recorded in a structured way.
  • • Work was still unfinished, including outstanding touch-up / finishing works.
  • • Contractor could not continue as available project funds became insufficient, while the homeowner was unwilling to commit further payment after RM420,000 had already been spent.
COMMON FAILURE POINT

Budget → Work → Progress → Claim → Payment → VO were not maintained as one structured record.

The issue is not necessarily that either party intends to mislead the other. Without a shared structured reference, the homeowner can feel overpaid while the contractor can feel underfunded — with the project still incomplete.

FROM COSTING TO PROJECT CONTROL

Cost transparency can become a shared reference that helps protect both sides.

The next development layer is to connect structured costing with work programme and S-curve progress monitoring.

01 · Cost

Budget → item → quantity → rate → amount.

02 · Work Programme

Activity, sequence, duration and planned timing.

03 · S-Curve

Planned progress/value compared with actual progress/value.

04 · Control

Cost vs progress vs payment, VO and remaining budget.

Principle: TERAJU WORKS is designed to create a shared financial and progress reference for homeowners and contractors — not to take the side of either party.
