04 · THE PROBLEM
Construction decisions can become fragmented between cost, work, progress, claim and payment.
Cost structure

Quantities, rates and scope can be difficult to maintain consistently when the starting point is only a lump-sum figure.

Project execution

Work programme, actual progress and payment can become separate records rather than one connected project reference.

Variations

Changes in scope and VO can become difficult to reconcile against the original budget.

Shared visibility

Homeowner and contractor may not have the same structured view of budget, work, progress and remaining value.

Problem04 / 17
05 · PROBLEM EVIDENCE
Real construction scenarios show why a connected reference matters.
CASE 01 · CLUSTER HOUSE · TAMAN DESIRAN BAYU
RM250k lump-sum contract

Claims were made by percentage without clear item-level justification. The homeowner had paid more than 95% while physical progress was below 70%. VO and scope changes became difficult to reconcile and the project reached a funding gap before completion.

CASE 02 · BUNGALOW · PENANG
RM300k budget → RM420k spent

RM420k had been spent — RM120k above the original budget (+40%). Work remained unfinished, including touch-up / finishing items. The contractor could not continue as funds became insufficient, while the homeowner was unwilling to commit further payment.

Common failure point

Budget → Work → Progress → Claim → Payment → VO were not maintained as one shared structured record. These are problem scenarios, not claims about TERAJUWORKS customers.
